Make a Space Shooter

In this tutorial you'll make a space shooter: a ship that flies over moving stars, shoots, and fights waves of enemies that shoot back, with explosions, lives, and a title screen.

It works like the platformer tutorial: every step has a game you can play, with its code right under it. Change the code and press Run to try it, or Reset code to put it back. If you haven't made a game with MDog Engine before, start with that one first.

In these boxes, MDog Engine is already imported and the screen is set to 256 by 192. In your own game, start with the import line from Getting Started.

1. A Ship and Stars

The ship sits near the bottom of the screen and slides left and right. Its position is a Vector for its middle, and it's drawn as a triangle with polygonFill(), which takes a list of corners.

The stars are what make it feel like flying. Each star is a small object with a position and a speed, and every tick they all move down. A star that falls off the bottom goes back to the top at a new spot, so there are always stars. Fast stars are drawn brighter, so they look closer.

Try making 200 stars, or making them fall faster.

2. Shooting

Each bullet is a Vector in a list. Pressing space adds one at the tip of the ship, and every tick each bullet moves up. Bullets that leave the top of the screen are taken out of the list, so it doesn't fill up forever.

Holding space keeps shooting, but not every tick, or it would be a solid laser. A cooldown counts down after each shot, and the ship can only shoot again once it's at 0. Here it's 20 ticks, so 8 shots a second (see Timers and Cooldowns).

The loop that moves the bullets goes backwards through the list. Taking something out of a list moves everything after it back by one, and going backwards means those are things the loop has already looked at.

Try changing the cooldown to 5, or shooting two bullets at once, one from each wing.

3. Enemies

Now there's something to shoot. Every 70 ticks, an enemy appears above the screen at a random spot and drifts down.

To tell when a bullet hits an enemy, both get a kickbox, which is MDog Engine's name for a hitbox (see Basics). A SquareKickbox is a rectangle attached to a Vector, so it moves along with it. Each bullet and enemy is now an object with a position and a box, made by makeBullet() and makeEnemy(). When a bullet's box is colliding() with an enemy's, both are taken out and the score goes up.

The score is drawn with textImage() and the marsfont.png pixel font. In your own game, the font image goes in your assets folder (see Add Text).

The break stops checking bullets once one has hit, since that enemy is gone. Try making the enemies come faster, or making them wider.

4. Explosions

Enemies just disappearing doesn't feel like much. Now each one explodes into a burst of particles from the FX Module: square chunks in fire colors flying out in every direction. Each chunk's direction is a Vector turned a random amount with rotate().

The screen also shakes for a moment. translate() shifts everything drawn after it, so shifting by a small random amount each tick makes the screen shake. The score is drawn after translate(0, 0), so it stays still.

Try a bigger shake, or more chunks. Little things like these are a big part of what makes a game feel good to play.

5. Waves

Instead of one enemy at a time, enemies now come in waves. Each wave flies in from the top into rows, then the whole group sways side to side together. Once every enemy in a wave is gone, the next wave starts, with two more enemies than the last.

Each enemy remembers its homeX and homeY, its spot in the rows. It flies down until it reaches homeY, and its x is its homeX plus a sway made with Math.sin(), which goes smoothly back and forth between -1 and 1.

The enemies also shoot back now, a little more each wave. Their bullets can't hurt you yet. That's the next step.

Try making the sway wider by changing the 24, or faster by changing the 90.

6. Lives and Game Over

Last, the enemies' bullets can hit the ship. The ship gets its own kickbox, and each hit takes one of its three lives. After a hit, the ship can't be hit again for a second, and it blinks so you can tell. Blinking is just not drawing the ship on some ticks.

When the last life is gone, the ship explodes and the game switches to a game over screen. Like in the platformer, each screen is its own function, setActiveFunction() switches between them, and startGame() puts everything back so you can play again. The stars keep moving on every screen, so they're in a function all the screens use.

What's Next

That's a whole space shooter! Here are some ideas to try next:

  • Add power-ups that fall from exploded enemies, like a double shot or an extra life.
  • Make some enemies dive down at the ship instead of staying in their rows.
  • Save the high score with localStorage (see Saving Progress).
