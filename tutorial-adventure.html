Make a Top-Down Adventure

In this tutorial you'll make a top-down adventure game, seen from above: the warrior explores rooms with walls and water, finds keys to open locked doors, fights slimes with her sword, and searches for treasure.

This is the biggest of the tutorials, and it uses ideas from the others, like kickboxes, animations, particles, and screens, so try the platformer tutorial first. Every step has a game you can play, with its code right under it. Change the code and press Run to try it, or Reset code to put it back.

In these boxes, MDog Engine is already imported and the screen is set to 256 by 192. In your own game, start with the import line from Getting Started. The tiles are from tiles.png in the Asset Packs.

1. Walking Around

The room is a TilemapInteractable from the UI Module: a grid of 16 by 16 tiles, made from rows of tile numbers. In tiles.png, 1 is dirt, 2 is stone, and 3 is water. Tile 0 is grass with dirt under it, made for side-on games like the platformer, so it isn't used here. Grass is an empty tile, -1, with the green background showing through.

Rows of numbers are hard to read, so the room is written as rows of letters instead, one letter per tile, that look like the room: . is grass, = is dirt, # is stone, and ~ is water. makeRoom() turns each letter into its tile number and makes the tilemap. The room is 16 tiles wide and 12 tall, which fills the 256 by 192 screen exactly.

Seen from above, the player walks in four directions. Each tick, the arrow keys being held make a direction, like (1, -1) for right and up. That direction is longer going diagonally than going straight, which would make diagonal walking faster. setLength() makes it the same length either way, so the player always walks at the same speed.

Try changing some of the letters in the room, then press Run to see it change.

2. Walls

Now stone and water stop the player. To tell if the player is in a wall, hitsWall() looks at the tiles under each corner of its kickbox. Dividing a position by 16 and rounding down gives which tile it's in, and get() gives that tile's number.

Moving works like in the platformer: first move across, and if that put the player in a wall, move back. Then the same going up and down. Doing them one at a time means the player can slide along a wall while walking into it diagonally, instead of getting stuck.

Checking only the corners works as long as the kickbox is no bigger than a tile. Try making dirt a wall too, by adding || tile === 1 in hitsWall().

3. The Warrior

Time to swap the square for the warrior from the Asset Packs. She uses her idle animation while standing and her run animation while walking, flipped with flipX when she faces left. Walking up or down keeps her facing the way she was.

Seen from above, things are standing on the ground, so only their feet should bump into walls. Her kickbox is now just her feet, 12 by 8, and her picture is drawn above it. That way her head can go in front of a wall behind her, which makes the room feel like it has depth.

To see her kickbox, try drawing a rectangle at playerBox.getLeftX(), playerBox.getTopY() that's 12 by 8, after she's drawn.

4. Keys and Doors

Now the way to the right side of the room is locked. The door is a kickbox that counts as a wall in blocked(), which checks the walls and every door. A key lies on the left side, and walking over it picks it up.

To open the door, the warrior needs to be right next to it with a key. A second kickbox, reachBox, is attached to the same Vector as her feet but is 2 pixels bigger on every side, so it touches things she's standing against. When it touches a door and she has a key, the key is used up and the door is taken out of the list.

Behind the door is a treasure chest. Walking into it opens it, and that's how you win.

Try adding a second door, with a second key to open it.

5. Slimes and a Sword

The room isn't safe anymore. Slimes wander around, and when the warrior gets close, they chase her. A slime's direction toward her is her position minus its own, set to the slime's speed with setLength(). Slimes move with the same move() as her, so walls stop them too. It now takes the position and kickbox to move.

Touching a slime takes one of her three hearts, knocks her back, and makes her blink for a moment while she can't be hurt again. Losing every heart starts the game over.

Press space to swing her sword. While the attack animation plays she stands still, and during the frames where the sword is out, a kickbox in front of her defeats any slime it touches.

Now that things can be in front of each other, they're drawn in order from the top of the screen to the bottom. Things lower on the screen are closer, so they're drawn last, on top.

Try making the slimes faster, or giving them more room to notice her by changing the 70.

6. Rooms to Explore

Last, the world gets bigger. There are now three rooms, and walking out through a gap in a room's edge goes into the room next to it. Rooms are named by where they are in a grid, like "1,0" for one room to the right of the start. When her middle goes past the right edge, the game switches to the room one to the right and moves her back by the width of the screen, so she's at its left edge.

Each room keeps its own tilemap, keys, doors, slimes, and chest, all made from roomData by makeRooms(). A slime you defeated stays gone if you leave and come back. To find the treasure, you'll need both keys.

The game also has a title screen, a game over screen, and a winning screen, which work like the ones in the other tutorials. startGame() makes the rooms again, so everything comes back for a new game.

What's Next

That's a whole adventure game! Here are some ideas to try next:

  • Add more rooms to roomData. Just make sure the gaps in the edges line up with the room next door.
  • Put hearts in some rooms that give back a heart when she walks over them.
  • Make the rooms in a spreadsheet and load them from .csv files (see Levels From a File).

You've finished every tutorial! The Playground is a good place to make your own game, and the docs under Engine in the sidebar have everything else MDog Engine can do.
