A Camera That Follows the Player

When a level is bigger than the screen, the camera has to follow the player around. MDog Engine doesn't have a camera object. Instead, MDog.Draw.translate() shifts everything that's drawn after it, so the world moves instead of the camera.

Run right with the arrow keys. The camera eases along behind the player, and stops at the ends of the level.

How it Works

Positions in the game are "world" positions, so the player's x goes all the way from 0 to the end of the level. cameraX is the world position of the left edge of the screen.

To keep the player in the middle, the camera's left edge goes half a screen to the left of the player. The camera is kept between 0 and the level's width minus the screen's width, so it never shows anything past the ends of the level.

translate(-cameraX, 0) shifts everything drawn after it to the left by cameraX, which makes the world scroll. Then translate(0, 0) puts things back to normal, for anything that should stay put, like the score.

Smooth or Locked

lerp() moves the camera part of the way to where it wants to be every tick, so it catches up smoothly. For a camera that's locked to the player instead, skip the lerp():

cameraX = player.x + 6 - MDog.Draw.getHalfScreenWidthInArtPixels();

The camera can end up between two pixels, so it's rounded with Math.round() before it's used. Without that, things can wobble by a pixel as the camera moves.

Up and Down Too

For a level that's tall as well as wide, do the same thing with a cameraY, and shift both ways at once:

MDog.Draw.translate(-Math.round(cameraX), -Math.round(cameraY));

Mouse positions from MDog.Input.Mouse are screen positions, so add cameraX and cameraY to them to find where the mouse is in the world.
