Make a 3D Racer

In this tutorial you'll make a retro 3D racing game, like the arcade racers from the 80s: a curvy road, a car you drive, trees flying past, other cars to pass, and lap times to beat.

It uses the ThreeDee Module, which draws flat shapes with perspective, so far away things look smaller. Every step has a game you can play, with its code right under it. Change the code and press Run to try it, or Reset code to put it back. If you haven't made a game with MDog Engine before, start with the platformer tutorial.

In these boxes, MDog Engine is already imported and the screen is set to 256 by 192. In your own game, start with the import line from Getting Started.

1. A Road

In 3D, positions have a third number, z, for how far away something is. The camera looks forward along z, so things with a bigger z are farther ahead. Driving is just moving the camera forward by adding to its z every tick.

The road is made of stripes, each 4 long, in two colors. That's what makes it look like it's moving. Every tick the game makes a new ThreeDeeScene with only the stripes that can be seen, from 3 in front of the camera to 100 away. Each stripe is a ThreeDeeFloor of grass across the whole screen, with the road on top made of ThreeDeeShapes, which are flat shapes made from 3D corners.

A scene draws things in the order they were added, so the farthest stripes are added first and the closer ones are drawn on top of them.

Try making the stripes longer, or the road wider by changing the 6s.

2. Your Car

Now there's a car to drive. Its position is a Vector3, and it moves forward by its speed every tick. Holding up speeds it up, letting go slows it down a little, and holding down brakes. Driving on the grass slows it down too.

The camera stays 5 behind the car and right in line with it, so the car is always in the middle of the screen, and the road moves around it instead. The car is a few ThreeDeeRectangles, which are rectangles that face the camera: wheels, a body, a window, and tail lights, each added after the one it covers.

Steering turns more the faster the car goes, so it can't slide sideways while it's stopped.

Try changing maxSpeed, or making the car a different color.

3. Curves and Trees

A straight road gets boring fast. roadCenter() now curves the road back and forth with two Math.sin() waves added together, so the curves aren't all the same. Since every stripe asks roadCenter() where it goes, the whole road follows. Now you have to steer to stay on it.

Trees stand beside the road every 10, on alternating sides, so you can feel how fast you're going. Trees and the car can be in front of or behind each other, so they're all put in one list, sorted from farthest to closest, then added to the scene in that order. The road is flat, so it can all be added first, under everything.

Try making the curves sharper by changing the 14, or putting trees on both sides.

4. Other Cars

A race needs someone to race. Six other cars drive along the road, each in a lane and at its own speed, a little slower than your top speed. A car's lane is how far it is from the middle of the road, so it follows the curves too.

There are only six cars, but the road feels busy because they're reused. A car that falls far behind you is moved far ahead, past where you can see, and comes back as if it were a new car. If you're the slow one, cars that get far ahead are moved back behind you.

Bumping into the back of a car puts you right behind it and slows you down. Cars only need to be close in x and z to crash, since they're all on the ground.

Try adding more colors to the list for more cars, or making them faster.

5. Laps and Times

The road now goes around in a loop, 600 long. The trick is that roadCenter() uses waves that repeat exactly every 600, so every lap has the same curves. A checkered finish line on the road and a banner over it mark where each lap starts.

The lap you're on is the car's z divided by the lap length, rounded down, plus one. When that number goes up, a lap just ended, so the game remembers how long it took and starts timing the next one. Times are counted in ticks, and there are 160 ticks in a second, so dividing by 160 gives seconds.

Try making lapLength shorter for quicker laps. Since the waves are made from it, the track still loops.

6. Start Lights and Finish

Last, the race gets a beginning and an end. The title screen shows the road slowly driving by. Pressing space puts everyone on the starting line, and a countdown of 3, 2, 1, GO holds the cars still until it's done. After 3 laps the race is over, the results show your total time and best lap, and your car coasts to a stop.

Like the other tutorials, each screen is its own function, and setActiveFunction() switches between them. drive() now takes whether the keys work, so the title and results screens can use it too, with nobody at the wheel.

What's Next

That's a whole racing game! Here are some ideas to try next:

  • Add hills by changing groundY along the road, the same way roadCenter() changes x.
  • Push the car toward the outside of curves, so steering through them takes more skill.
  • Save your best lap with localStorage (see Saving Progress), and show it on the title screen.

For the last and biggest one, try the adventure game tutorial.
